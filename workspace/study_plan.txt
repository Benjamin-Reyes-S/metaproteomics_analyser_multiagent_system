Study design case: condition_comparison

This study compares metaproteomic profiles between diseased and control conditions across multiple independent cohorts. The analysis will focus on differential protein abundance while accounting for batch effects from the merging of independent studies. Given the protein-centric annotation philosophy, quantification is at the protein group level. The plan outlines QC, filtering, normalization, batch correction, and differential abundance modeling, with explicit handling of missing values and host/microbial separation.

QC PLAN
- host/microbial matrices separated: True
- check: sample IDs match metadata
- check: total intensity per sample
- check: missingness % per sample
- check: PCA by condition/batch/study
- check: RLE plots
- check: technical-replicate CV (if any)
- check: per-taxon/function coverage
- flag: technical-replicate CV > 0.2 within a condition/batch
- flag: high keratin/contaminant fraction
- flag: samples with missingness > 50%
- flag: outliers in PCA driven by batch rather than condition

FILTERING PLAN
- remove contaminants/decoys: True
- minimum unique peptides: 1
- prevalence cutoff: 20.0% (within_group)
- filter host/microbial separately: True
- rationale: Host and microbial proteins have distinct abundance distributions and biological roles; filtering separately prevents host proteins from dominating prevalence thresholds. Contaminants/decoys must be removed to avoid false positives. Prevalence filtering within groups ensures we retain proteins consistently detected in at least one condition, which is critical for differential abundance power.

NORMALIZATION PLAN
- intensity method: vsn
- compositional method: none
- rationale: Continuous LFQ intensities require variance-stabilizing normalization. VSN is robust to outliers and handles the heteroscedasticity common in mass spectrometry data. Compositional methods are inappropriate for continuous intensity matrices.

BATCH ASSESSMENT AND CORRECTION
- confounded with biology: False
- correction method: covariate_in_model
- rationale: Batch effects from merged studies are expected and not confounded with condition. Modeling batch as a covariate in the differential abundance model is preferred over irreversible matrix transformation to preserve biological variance and allow flexible adjustment for multiple known confounders (study, UseCase).
- validation: PCA before/after correction
- validation: variance explained by batch vs biology
- validation: technical-replicate CVs
- validation: check that condition effect is not attenuated

DIFFERENTIAL ABUNDANCE PLAN
- applicable: True
- model type: limma_moderated
- model formula: abundance ~ condition + study + UseCase
- paired: False
- missing-value policy: Filter proteins with prevalence < 20% in at least one group before imputation. For remaining missing values, use left-censored imputation (e.g., narrow normal distribution below observed minimum) rather than global minimum, as missingness is likely abundance-dependent (MNAR).
- rationale: Limma's empirical Bayes moderation improves power and stability for proteomics data with limited replicates. The formula accounts for the primary condition of interest while adjusting for known batch (study) and potential technical grouping (UseCase). Explicit MNAR-aware imputation prevents bias from abundance-dependent missingness.
- must report: log2 fold change
- must report: 95% CIs
- must report: raw and FDR-adjusted p-values
- must report: number of observations supporting each protein/function

VISUALIZATION PLAN
- protein level: volcano plots
- protein level: heatmaps of top DA proteins
- protein level: PCA/t-SNE colored by condition and batch
- taxonomic level: bar plots of relative abundance by kingdom/phylum
- taxonomic level: taxonomic composition heatmaps
- functional level: bar plots of main role/subrole abundance
- functional level: functional enrichment dot plots

ANNOTATION PHILOSOPHY IMPACT
Protein-centric annotation means peptides are collapsed prior to quantification, improving protein identification confidence but potentially masking isoform-specific or strain-level variation. Taxonomic resolution will be limited to the protein group level (often genus or species), and functional annotations will reflect protein-level roles rather than peptide-specific pathways. This is appropriate for condition-level DA but may require downstream peptide-level validation for fine-scale resolution.
